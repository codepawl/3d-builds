# Fruit fly (Drosophila melanogaster), female, resting, seen from above

Written before drawing, as the skill asks. Sources: Robertson 1952 (thorax and wing lengths), the photo
"Drosophila melanogaster - top (aka).jpg" by André Karwath on Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.5), and general insect
anatomy. Each number is marked measured (a source), or inferred (read off the photo or general knowledge).

Scale: 1 mm = 240 px.

## Parts, what they are made of, where they attach

| Part | Built from | Attaches to | Size | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Head | head capsule; two compound eyes on its sides; frons strip between the eyes; three ocelli on the vertex; two antennae at the front | front of the thorax by a narrow neck | head plus eyes about as wide as the thorax (inferred, photo) | eyes bright red; frons and capsule orange-brown |
| Compound eye | about 750 to 800 ommatidia (inferred), a hexagon mosaic | covers most of each side of the head | from above each eye about a third of the head width; frons strip about a third (inferred) | red, a paler patch where light falls, a dark pseudopupil |
| Antenna | a short rounded segment and a feathered bristle (arista) | front of the frons, between the eyes | arista about 0.25 mm (inferred) | small; points forward and out |
| Thorax | one large hump (mesonotum) and the scutellum behind it | carries all six legs underneath and both wings at the shoulders | about 1.0 mm long to the scutellum tip (measured); longer than wide (photo) | orange-brown, darker toward the back, rows of stiff black bristles |
| Wing | a clear membrane stiffened by longitudinal veins; narrow at the hinge, widest about two thirds along, round tip | a hinge on the outer side of the thorax just in front of the scutellum, not near the midline; folded, the leading edge lies outside and the trailing edge crosses the midline over the other wing | about 2.0 mm, twice the thorax (measured) | almost clear; rainbow film strongest where the two wings overlap; veins thin and brown |
| Abdomen | about six visible segments (tergites), each with a dark band at its rear edge | the back of the thorax | about 1.3 to 1.5 mm in a female (inferred) | tan with dark brown bands, darker and more pointed at the tip |
| Leg | coxa, trochanter, femur, tibia, five tarsomeres, two claws | under the thorax near the midline, three per side | femur and tibia about 0.5 mm each (inferred) | brown, thin, bristles on femur and tibia |

## How the parts relate when the fly rests

- The wings fold flat over the back and overlap each other along the midline like closed scissors. They cover most of
  the abdomen, which shows only through the clear membrane, and their tips reach past the abdomen's end (photo,
  matching the 2 mm wing).
- The legs stand under the body: from above you see the femurs and tibias of each pair angle out, and the tarsi touch
  down just outside the body's outline. Front legs point forward, middle legs out, hind legs back. Every knee is the
  outermost point of its leg and bends toward the body.
- The head sits lower than the thorax hump and a little narrower; the neck is hidden.

## How the parts move

- Wings: closed at rest. In flight they beat about 200 times a second (inferred, common figure), so they show as a blur.
- Legs: a walking fly lifts three legs at a time (front and hind of one side with the middle of the other), the
  alternating tripod gait.
- Head: turns a little left and right; antennae twitch.
- Abdomen: pulses slowly with breathing; bends down to lay eggs.

## Differences from Kevin Ngo's frame

His fly holds the wings slightly apart so the banded abdomen shows, and splays the legs wide. That reads well as an
illustration but is not the resting pose in the photo. We follow the photo unless An asks for the stylised pose.
