# Fruit fly egg and early embryo, the blueprint beat (about 5 s)

Written before drawing. Sources: Drosophila embryogenesis on Wikipedia (its "Pair rule" figure, eve and en stripes),
Karr et al. 1985 and 1989 (Kornberg lab), PMC3616919. Measured = in a source; inferred = general knowledge or a photo.

Scale: 1 µm = 1.52 px (the egg's 500 µm length is 760 px).

## Parts

| Part | What it is | Size | Look in blueprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg (chorion) | shell with a faint polygon imprint left by the follicle cells | about 500 x 180 µm (inferred); one long side flatter (dorsal), the other rounder (ventral, Wikipedia figure) | light line, faint hexagon print |
| Dorsal appendages | two breathing filaments on the dorsal side of the front end, flattened paddle tips | about 200 to 300 µm (inferred) | two thin lines with paddles |
| Nuclei, cycles 1 to 7 | divide in sync inside the yolk | 1, 2, 4 ... 128 | glowing dots that split from their parent |
| Nuclei, cycles 8 to 13 | move to the surface: the syncytial blastoderm; about 100 yolk nuclei stay inside | about 6000 at the surface by cycle 13 (measured) | a dense layer just inside the shell; drawn as about 900 dots |
| Pole cells | bud first, at the posterior end | about 45 nuclei (measured) | a small cluster bulging out at the back, warm colour |
| Cellularization | cycle 14: membranes grow down between the surface nuclei | | short ticks between the surface dots |
| even-skipped | pair-rule gene, 7 narrow stripes across the long axis in the trunk (measured) | trunk only, not head or tail | green stripes |
| engrailed | 14 one-cell-wide stripes by gastrulation, one per segment's back edge (measured) | trunk only | magenta stripes, thinner, between and over the eve stripes |

## How a nucleus divides (live imaging)

Measured from two time-lapse videos on YouTube: "Early mitotic divisions in a Drosophila embryo" (tglab) and
"Mitotic Waves in Drosophila" (Stefano Di Talia's lab), watched frame by frame at 8 fps.

1. Interphase: round nuclei, evenly spaced.
2. The nucleus condenses into a short bright bar (chromosomes on the metaphase plate). Each bar has its own angle;
   neighbours point in similar directions.
3. The bar splits into two that pull apart along the spindle, stretching into streaks; faint spindle rays at the poles.
4. The two daughters round up into smaller dots and push apart until the sheet is even again, now twice as dense.
5. Divisions run as a wave along the embryo, not all at once.

## Order and timing in the beat

0.0 to 0.4 s shell draws in; 0.4 to 2.0 s cycles 1 to 7 inside; 2.0 to 2.8 s nuclei move out to the surface and
multiply, pole cells bud; 2.8 to 3.4 s cellularization ticks; 3.4 to 4.2 s seven eve stripes; 4.2 to 5.0 s fourteen
en stripes. The real timeline is about 3 hours to cycle 14 (inferred); a cycle counter shows where we are.
