# Motion references

Consulted on 2026-10-10 for the wall transition and descending jump revision.

- Nabawy et al. (2018), [Energy and time optimal trajectories in exploratory jumps of the spider Phidippus regius](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-25227-9).
  High-speed measurements show task-dependent trajectories; short jumps use
  lower angles than longer or elevated jumps. Leg support produces takeoff
  acceleration, rather than an instantaneous change of velocity.
- [University of Manchester research summary](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/scientists-train-spider-to-jump-on-demand-to-discover-secrets-of-animal-movement/), 8 May 2018.
  Describes the controlled height/distance experiments and their high-speed capture.

Application to this stylized six-legged machine: overlap the two leading feet's
transfer instead of stopping both at every waypoint, keep the hull moving with
its support, match launch velocity to flight, and retain body-relative foot
targets until the landing approach. This is an animation adaptation, not a
biological reconstruction. The user's forward heading and top-surface grip remain.
